# NVIDIA — 12-question defense bank

Nov 12 local revision · 18 numbered slides · use for rehearsal and Q&A.

Case facts are historical. Page references below follow the supplied Nov 12 build spec; the original case PDF and WAC DOCX were not available in this checkout. Proposal and tier labels are team judgments, not observed outcomes.

## 01. Is NVIDIA still in a blue ocean?

By results yes, by structure no. Moves 1–2 defend today’s market; Move 3 looks for the next one.

**Slides:** 6, 12 · **Lead:** Group 2

## 02. Why would a 10% discount matter if Google’s chip costs 70–80% less?

It doesn’t for work their chip can already do. It protects the jobs their chips can’t do yet, mostly training, and only with a purchase commitment.

**Slides:** 11 · **Lead:** Group 2

## 03. Wasn’t DeepSeek the real threat?

DeepSeek-V3 used NVIDIA H800s. Its reported training-run estimate excludes earlier R&D and is not the complete cost of building DeepSeek-R1. Cheaper AI may expand usage, but our case recommendation addresses substitution by NVIDIA’s largest buyers.

**Slides:** 1 · **Lead:** Group 1

## 04. Why not buy rivals or partners?

Regulators blocked the $40B ARM deal and are reviewing Run.ai (p. 17).

**Slides:** 13, 15 · **Lead:** Group 3

## 05. Why reject exclusive contracts?

Regulators already watch NVIDIA, and a contract doesn’t stop a buyer from building its own chip.

**Slides:** 13 · **Lead:** Group 3

## 06. Can’t Google and Amazon build whole systems themselves?

Yes. Move 2 is for mixed fleets: they still buy NVIDIA at scale (Meta, ~350,000 H100s in 2024, p. 14). That’s why it is “test first”.

**Slides:** 13, 17 · **Lead:** Group 3

## 07. How big is AI Foundry today?

The case gives AI Foundry no revenue figure (poll Q10 = D, not yet proven). We do not have a separately disclosed figure in the supplied materials; we do not claim an exhaustive search of all later disclosures.

**Slides:** 6, 12 · **Lead:** Group 3

## 08. Why an insurer?

It is an example of a tier 2 noncustomer: document-heavy manual work and no in-house chip team. It is our proposal; if it doesn’t convert, we try another segment.

**Slides:** 12 · **Lead:** Group 3

## 09. What about China and Huawei?

US export limits cut China sales; Alibaba, ByteDance and Baidu now buy Huawei’s Ascend (p. 12, 17). It is outside our three moves, and we watch it.

**Slides:** Q&A · **Lead:** Group 3

## 10. What about TSMC?

At the case date, TSMC’s leading-node capacity and ASML’s EUV tools were supply constraints. This is a case-period risk, not a claim about availability in November 2026. The three moves do not create a second leading-edge supplier.

**Slides:** Q&A · **Lead:** Group 2

## 11. Why no inference slide?

The decision rule is per workload: move when the buyer’s chip can do the job well enough for less. Inference is one early example, not an exclusive category. The discount protects jobs their chips cannot yet perform competitively, mainly frontier training.

**Slides:** 10 · **Lead:** Group 2

## 12. Is the 40% top-4 share still true?

It is the case’s 2023 figure. We didn’t replace it with later data.

**Slides:** 4 · **Lead:** Group 1
