# NVIDIA Case: Presenter Talking Points

> Working reference for the NVIDIA Case Keynote. Keep slide copy, speaker notes, click cues, and this script aligned when making future changes.

ECO 502 · Group presentation · Nov 14, 2026 · For the deck “NVIDIA Case Keynote” (16 slides + 2 backup)

## How to read this script

Italic text in square brackets is a delivery cue. Do it, don’t say it.

Plain text is what you say. **Bold** words get extra stress.

Each slide ends with a **Key line**: if you forget everything else on that slide, say this.

## Five delivery rules

- One idea per breath. Every sentence here is short on purpose. Don’t join them together.

- Pause after every big number. Silence is what makes the audience remember it.

- Talk to the room, not the screen. Glance at the slide, then turn back.

- Explain every technical word the first time. The script already does this; don’t skip those lines.

- Hand off out loud. The last line of each group is a question the next speaker answers.

## Slide 1 of 16: Hook

**Presenter:** Group 1  
**Estimated time:** ~1.5 min

**On screen:** On one day in January 2025, NVIDIA lost $600 billion in market value. Was cheaper AI the real threat?

### Speaker script

*[Walk to the centre of the room. Say nothing. Let the number sit on screen for three seconds.]*

Six hundred billion dollars.

That is how much NVIDIA’s market value fell. In one trading day. January 2025.

*[Pause.]*

And apparently, things haven't been going too well for its CEO, Jensen Huang, either.

*[CLICK: Jensen Huang meme appears.]*

Well... not exactly. But even the “king of AI” isn't safe from a controversy.

But let's put the headlines aside for a moment. Because that $600 billion tells a much bigger story.

*[Slow down. Let each fact land.]*

Market value is what all of a company’s shares are worth together. So in a single day, investors decided NVIDIA was worth $600 billion less.

*[CLICK: The meme leaves. DeepSeek appears with “<$6M vs $100M+”.]*

What happened? A small Chinese lab called DeepSeek said it trained a top AI model for under $6 million. Big US labs spend over $100 million.

Investors panicked. Their logic was simple. If AI gets this cheap, the world needs fewer NVIDIA chips.

*[Click. The question appears. Look around the room.]*

So here is our question for the next thirty minutes. **Was cheaper AI the real threat?**

*[Pause two seconds. Shake your head slightly.]*

We say no. The real threat is much closer to home. It is on NVIDIA’s own customer list.

To see it, we first need to know how NVIDIA got so big.

**Key line:** The real threat is on NVIDIA’s own customer list.

## Slide 2 of 16: Case facts

**Presenter:** Group 1  
**Estimated time:** ~2 min

**On screen:** From gaming chips to $3 trillion  
**Clicks:** 3 (1999 → 2007 with the CUDA logo, then ChatGPT, then the market value bars)

### Speaker script

*[Point to the timeline and move your hand left to right.]*

Rewind to 1999. NVIDIA makes the first GPU.

A GPU, or graphics processing unit, is a chip that does thousands of small calculations at the same time. It was built to make video games look real.

*[CLICK 1: The timeline moves to 2007 and the CUDA logo appears.]*

In 2007, NVIDIA releases CUDA. CUDA is software that lets programmers use those gaming chips for any heavy number-crunching. Not just games.

*[Step toward the screen. Raise your energy.]*

*[CLICK 2: ChatGPT appears on the timeline.]*

Then, November 2022. ChatGPT launches. A hundred million people try it within two months. Every big company suddenly wants its own AI.

And training AI needs exactly what a GPU does best. Millions of small calculations, all at once.

*[CLICK 3: Market value bars grow. Point to them.]*

NVIDIA’s value goes from about $400 billion to over $3 trillion. In about two years.

In fiscal 2024, revenue grows 126%. Data center revenue grows 217%.

A data center is a building full of computers that companies use to run software at scale. That is where AI lives.

*[Pause. Ask the room, as if you really want an answer.]*

So how did one company capture so much of this boom? Because NVIDIA does not just sell a chip.

**Key line:** NVIDIA does not just sell a chip.

**Note for Q&A:** If asked: “fiscal 2024” is NVIDIA’s financial year ending January 2024.

## Slide 3 of 16: Case facts

**Presenter:** Group 1  
**Estimated time:** ~2 min

**On screen:** The cost of leaving  
**Clicks:** 6 (one per loop step, then the switching cost panel)

### Speaker script

*[Trace the loop on screen with your hand, one step at a time.]*

Here is how NVIDIA makes money. It is a loop with five steps.

*[CLICK 1: Step 1 appears.]*

One. NVIDIA builds the fastest chips.

*[CLICK 2: Step 2 appears.]*

Two. CUDA makes those chips easy to program. It comes with over 300 ready-made libraries. A library is code someone already wrote, so you don’t have to.

*[CLICK 3: Step 3 appears.]*

Three. About five million developers learn CUDA. Their code runs best on NVIDIA.

*[CLICK 4: Step 4 appears.]*

Four. Buyers follow the developers. That gives NVIDIA pricing power: the ability to charge more without losing customers.

*[CLICK 5: Step 5 appears.]*

Five. Those profits pay for the next, faster chip. And the loop starts again.

*[Stop moving. Lower your voice slightly. This is the key idea of the whole talk.]*

*[CLICK 6: The switching cost panel appears.]*

Now here is the important part. A customer who wants to leave NVIDIA can’t just swap one chip for another.

It has to rewrite its software. Retrain its engineers. Give up those 300 libraries.

Economists call this a **switching cost**. Simply, the cost of leaving.

And that cost of leaving is what NVIDIA really sells.

*[One beat of silence. Then ask.]*

So who has enough money and engineers to pay that cost?

**Key line:** The cost of leaving is what NVIDIA really sells.

## Slide 4 of 16: Case facts

**Presenter:** Group 1  
**Estimated time:** ~1.5 min

**On screen:** Their biggest customers are building their own chips  
**Clicks:** 1 (each company’s own chip)

### Speaker script

*[Gesture to each company as it lights up.]*

These four. Microsoft. Meta. Amazon. Google.

Together they bought about 40% of everything NVIDIA sold. That is roughly $24 billion a year.

*[Let that number sit for a moment.]*

*[CLICK 1: Each company’s own chip appears.]*

And every one of them is now building its own AI chip. Microsoft has MAIA. Meta has MTIA. Amazon has Trainium and Inferentia. Google has the TPU.

The industry calls them hyperscalers. Companies that run cloud computing at enormous scale.

*[Lean in. Make it slightly provocative.]*

They have the money. They have the engineers. They even have their own software.

Think about that. **NVIDIA’s biggest customers are building their own replacement for NVIDIA.**

That is our problem.

**Key line:** NVIDIA’s biggest customers are building their own replacement for NVIDIA.

## Slide 5 of 16: Problem

**Presenter:** Group 1  
**Estimated time:** ~1.5 min

**On screen:** Can NVIDIA keep its pricing power?  
**Clicks:** 3 (reason and means, the four questions, antitrust)

### Speaker script

*[Slow and deliberate. This is the thesis of the presentation.]*

Let me put the problem in one sentence.

NVIDIA charges premium prices because customers are locked into CUDA. And its four biggest customers now have both the reason and the means to break free.

*[CLICK 1: Reason and means appear.]*

The reason? NVIDIA’s high prices.

The means? Their size. Their own software. Their own clouds.

*[CLICK 2: The four questions appear. Point to them one at a time.]*

To decide what NVIDIA should do, we need four answers. How much rides on this business? Why can NVIDIA charge so much? Who can break the lock-in? And what is it worth to stop them?

*[CLICK 3: Antitrust appears.]*

One more limit. Antitrust. Those are the laws that stop a dominant company from blocking competition. Regulators are already watching NVIDIA.

*[Handoff. Turn and open your hand toward the Group 2 presenter.]*

My colleague will take us through the evidence. First question: how much does NVIDIA depend on this one business?

**Key line:** Four questions decide what NVIDIA should do.

## Slide 6 of 16: Analysis · Q1

**Presenter:** Group 2  
**Estimated time:** ~2.5 min

**On screen:** 78% of NVIDIA’s revenue now comes from one business, and it is NVIDIA’s only proven growth engine

### Speaker script

*[Point to the three bars, left to right.]*

Two years earlier, data centers were 39% of NVIDIA’s revenue. Then 56%. Now 78%.

Almost four out of every five dollars come from one business.

And it is very profitable. About 85 cents of every extra dollar of sales became operating profit. Operating profit is what is left after making the chips and running the company.

*[Move to the map on the right.]*

The case uses a Blue Ocean tool that sorts businesses into three groups. Pioneers offer value nobody offered before. Migrators are better than rivals, but in a crowded market. Settlers are me-too.

Today, the AI data center business is a pioneer. Gaming is a migrator. AI Foundry, NVIDIA’s new service that helps companies build their own AI models, might be the next pioneer. But the case shows no revenue for it yet.

*[Click. Let the box slide across.]*

Now watch what happens if NVIDIA stands still. Rivals catch up. The data center slides into the migrator column.

*[Pause. Point at the empty pioneer column.]*

And the pioneer column is empty.

That is why NVIDIA must act now, while it is still on top.

*[Transition. Curious tone.]*

Next question. Why can NVIDIA charge so much?

**Key line:** If NVIDIA stands still, it has no proven pioneer left.

## Slide 7 of 16: Analysis · Q2

**Presenter:** Group 2  
**Estimated time:** ~2 min

**On screen:** NVIDIA’s growth came mostly from higher prices, not more chips

### Speaker script

*[Point to the two numbers, one at a time.]*

Between fiscal 2023 and 2024, NVIDIA shipped 42% more data center chips.

But revenue per chip? Up 122%.

*[Pause.]*

Customers didn’t just buy more. They paid more than twice as much per chip.

*[Move to the margin bars.]*

Now look at gross margin. That is the share of each sales dollar left after paying to make the product.

NVIDIA: 73%. AMD: 47%. Intel: 41%.

Buyers have a name for that gap. They call it the **NVIDIA tax**.

*[Lean in. Provocative.]*

Here is the twist. That 73% is NVIDIA’s reward. It is also its weak spot. The bigger the tax, the stronger the reason for customers to build their own chips.

So what exactly are they paying for?

**Key line:** The 73% margin is NVIDIA’s reward and its weak spot.

## Slide 8 of 16: Analysis · Q2

**Presenter:** Group 2  
**Estimated time:** ~2 min

**On screen:** Buyers pay for the finished AI job, not for raw chip speed

### Speaker script

*[Playful. This slide surprises people. Set it up like a puzzle.]*

Here is a puzzle.

The newer H100 chip gives you 14% fewer raw calculations per dollar than the older A100. Raw speed is measured in TFLOPS, meaning trillions of calculations per second.

And it costs four times as much. About $40,000 each.

*[Ask the room. Wait one beat.]*

So why does everyone want it?

Because it finishes an AI training job about nine times faster. Training is when an AI model learns from huge amounts of data.

Nine times faster, for four times the price. That is about two and a quarter times more training for every dollar.

So buyers are not paying for a chip. They are paying for the **finished job**: chip, software and networking working together.

That is why customers waited up to 18 months for NVIDIA chips instead of buying from someone else.

*[Transition. Firmer voice.]*

So if the difficulty of switching is what lets NVIDIA earn 73%, the real question is: who is able to switch?

**Key line:** Buyers pay for the finished job, not the chip.

## Slide 9 of 16: Analysis · Q3

**Presenter:** Group 2  
**Estimated time:** ~2 min

**On screen:** NVIDIA’s biggest buyers are also its biggest substitutes

### Speaker script

*[Point to the highlighted label: SAME FOUR FIRMS.]*

We used Porter’s Five Forces, a tool from class. It shows who holds power in an industry: buyers, suppliers, rivals, new entrants and substitutes.

A substitute is a different product that does the same job.

*[Slow down. This is the main insight of the analysis.]*

In most industries, buyers and substitutes are different companies. Here, they are the same four.

Microsoft, Meta, Amazon and Google are NVIDIA’s biggest buyers. And their own chips are NVIDIA’s biggest substitutes.

*[Pick up the pace. Move quickly past the other forces.]*

AMD and Intel? Real rivals. But they mostly win when NVIDIA is sold out.

Suppliers? TSMC in Taiwan makes NVIDIA’s most advanced chips, and nobody else can make them yet. That is a risk to watch. It is not the core problem.

*[Firm.]*

The threat to NVIDIA is on its own customer list.

So what will these four do next?

**Key line:** The buyers and the substitutes are the same four firms.

## Slide 10 of 16: Analysis · Q3

**Presenter:** Group 2  
**Estimated time:** ~2 min

**On screen:** The big buyers will move their own inference work to their own chips and keep NVIDIA for the most advanced training

### Speaker script

*[Walk through the four corners in order.]*

To predict their next move, we used the Four Corners model. It looks at a competitor from four sides. What drives it. What it is doing now. What it believes. And what it can do.

Drivers: cut the NVIDIA tax. And never run short of chips again.

Current strategy: build their own chips. Rent them out through their clouds.

Belief: Google’s CEO said spending too little on AI is riskier than spending too much. So they will keep spending.

Capabilities: Amazon, Microsoft and Google hold 65% of the cloud market. Google’s own chip costs it 70 to 80% less than NVIDIA’s A100.

*[Click to reveal the prediction. Say it clearly.]*

So here is our prediction. They will move their everyday AI work, called inference, onto their own chips.

Inference is when a trained AI answers your question. It happens billions of times a day.

And they will keep NVIDIA only for the hardest job: training the most advanced models.

*[Handoff. Turn toward the Group 3 presenter.]*

So the big buyers will keep moving work away. How much is it worth to NVIDIA to slow that down? Over to my colleague.

**Key line:** Everyday AI work moves to their chips; NVIDIA keeps the hardest jobs.

## Slide 11 of 16: Analysis · Q4

**Presenter:** Group 3  
**Estimated time:** ~2 min

**On screen:** Losing about 1 in 6 dollars of top-buyer business would cost more than a targeted price cut

### Speaker script

*[Be upfront. Point to the ASSUMPTION tag.]*

This is the only slide built on assumptions. So we labelled it, and we will let you test it.

The top four buy about $24 billion a year. NVIDIA makes almost $18 billion of gross profit on that.

Option A. NVIDIA cuts its margin for these four, from 73% to 60%. Cost: about $3.1 billion a year.

Option B. NVIDIA holds its price, and the four move a quarter of their buying to their own chips. Loss: about $4.4 billion a year.

*[Point to 17.5%.]*

The break-even is 17.5%. Break-even is the point where both options cost the same.

In plain words: if a price cut keeps more than **one in six dollars** from leaving, it pays for itself.

*[Optional: drag a slider if time allows.]*

One more thing. Once a customer’s own chip works, that business rarely comes back. A price cut can be reversed next year.

So which response should NVIDIA choose?

**Key line:** Lost business rarely comes back. A price cut can be reversed.

## Slide 12 of 16: Options

**Presenter:** Group 3  
**Estimated time:** ~1.5 min

**On screen:** Five possible responses, tested against what the analysis showed

### Speaker script

*[Steady and quick. Don’t read the table.]*

The case gives five possible responses. We tested each one with four questions. Does it protect the lock-in? Does it reduce reliance on the top four? Does it risk antitrust trouble? What does it cost in margin?

*[Point to the two rejected rows.]*

Two fail.

Exclusive contracts fail. Regulators are already watching NVIDIA. And a contract doesn’t stop a customer from building its own chip.

Selling NVIDIA’s chip design know-how fails too. It hands rivals the very advantage NVIDIA is trying to protect.

*[Point to the three kept rows.]*

Three pass. Together, they become our recommendation.

**Key line:** Two options fail. Three pass.

## Slide 13 of 16: Recommendation

**Presenter:** Group 3  
**Estimated time:** ~2.5 min

**On screen:** Three moves, and what NVIDIA gives up to make them

### Speaker script

*[Stand tall. Slow down. This is the answer.]*

Our recommendation in one line. **Make staying with NVIDIA the cheapest and fastest option.** Don’t try to make leaving impossible.

We used the ERRC grid, a Blue Ocean tool. It asks what to eliminate, raise, reduce and create.

Three moves.

Move one. Keep CUDA the fastest way to build AI. Even for developers who use PyTorch. PyTorch is free software many AI developers use, and it can run on other companies’ chips. So CUDA must stay the fastest option underneath it.

Move two. Keep the big buyers inside NVIDIA’s systems. Let their own chips plug into NVIDIA’s racks and networking. Offer semi-custom products, partly built to their needs. And cut prices for those four only where the numbers say it pays.

Move three. Grow customers who can’t build their own chips. Ordinary companies. Governments. Newer cloud companies.

*[Point to “We give up”. Honest and firm.]*

And here is what NVIDIA gives up. Exclusive deals. Big acquisitions. And its top margins on its four biggest buyers.

Every move answers one finding. Here is the proof.

**Key line:** Make staying with NVIDIA the cheapest and fastest option.

## Slide 14 of 16: Justification

**Presenter:** Group 3  
**Estimated time:** ~1.5 min

**On screen:** Each move answers one finding from the analysis

### Speaker script

*[Run your hand across one row to show how to read it.]*

Read any row from left to right. Finding, then move.

Buyers pay for the finished job. So NVIDIA protects the software that makes the job fast.

The big buyers are also the substitutes. So NVIDIA keeps their chips inside its own systems.

Losing business costs more than a targeted price cut. So NVIDIA prices selectively for those four.

Too much rides on one business and four buyers. So NVIDIA grows customers who can’t build their own chips.

*[Firm.]*

No move is on this slide without a finding behind it.

Now, what could break this plan?

**Key line:** No move without a finding behind it.

## Slide 15 of 16: Justification

**Presenter:** Group 3  
**Estimated time:** ~1.5 min

**On screen:** What could break the plan, and the early warning signs we would watch

### Speaker script

*[Brisk through the risks. Slow down for the weak spot.]*

Every plan can fail. Here is how ours could, and what we would watch.

Biggest risk: customers’ own chips get good enough faster than we expect. The warning sign: more of their AI work running on their own chips.

Others: free software making CUDA optional. Regulators. Chip supply at TSMC. And AI getting so cheap that spending slows.

*[Raise the weak spot yourself, before anyone asks. Look straight at the audience.]*

Now let us challenge ourselves. After the case, NVIDIA’s revenue kept growing. Its margin went back to about 75%. So is the threat overstated?

We don’t think so. Competition usually hits prices before it hits sales. And in-house chips take years to mature.

The time to act is while you are strong. Not after.

So let’s test our recommendation. Put yourself in Microsoft’s position.

**Key line:** Act while strong, not after.

**Note for Q&A:** The post-case figures (revenue growth, ~75% margin) are from outside the case. Say so if asked.

## Slide 16 of 16: Close

**Presenter:** Group 3  
**Estimated time:** ~1.5 min

**On screen:** If you were Microsoft, would you still build your own chip if it could plug into NVIDIA’s systems?

### Speaker script

*[Pause. Look at the room. Ask, then wait.]*

If you were Microsoft, would you still spend billions building your own chip, if that chip could plug straight into NVIDIA’s systems?

*[Take two or three answers. Don’t argue. Thank each person.]*

*[Click.]*

In May 2025, after this case was written, NVIDIA announced NVLink Fusion. NVLink is NVIDIA’s high-speed connection between chips. Fusion lets customers’ own chips run inside NVIDIA’s racks.

This is close to our Move 2. It does not prove the move works. But it shows NVIDIA chose it.

*[Click. Walk back to the spot where you started Slide 1.]*

So, back to that $600 billion.

That fall was about cheaper AI. NVIDIA’s lasting risk is about who builds the chips.

*[Slow. Final line. Then stop.]*

Make staying the best option, and the biggest buyers stay.

Thank you.

**Key line:** Make staying the best option, and the biggest buyers stay.

## Quick glossary: every technical term in the script

| Term | Plain meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Market value | What all of a company’s shares are worth together |

| Pricing power | The ability to charge more without losing customers |
| Switching cost | What a customer loses by changing supplier: rewriting code, retraining staff |
| Lock-in | When switching costs are so high that customers stay |
| Hyperscaler | A company that runs cloud computing at enormous scale (Microsoft, Amazon, Google, Meta) |
| Antitrust | Laws that stop a dominant company from blocking competition |
| Operating profit | What is left after paying to make the product and run the company |
| Gross margin | The share of each sales dollar left after paying to make the product |
| TFLOPS | Trillions of calculations per second; a measure of raw chip speed |
| Training | When an AI model learns from huge amounts of data |
| Inference | When a trained AI model answers a question; happens billions of times a day |
| Pioneer / migrator / settler | Blue Ocean labels: new value / better than rivals / me-too |
| Five Forces | Porter’s tool showing who holds power: buyers, suppliers, rivals, new entrants, substitutes |
| Substitute | A different product that does the same job |
| Four Corners | A tool to predict a competitor’s next move: drivers, strategy, beliefs, capabilities |
| Break-even | The point where two options cost the same |
| ERRC grid | Blue Ocean tool: what to eliminate, raise, reduce and create |
| PyTorch | Free AI programming software that can run on many companies’ chips |
| Semi-custom | A product partly built to one customer’s needs |
| TSMC | The Taiwanese company that manufactures NVIDIA’s most advanced chips |
| NVLink Fusion | NVIDIA’s 2025 product that lets customers’ own chips connect into NVIDIA systems (outside the case) |
